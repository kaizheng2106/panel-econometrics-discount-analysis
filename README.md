**This project investigates the causal effect of promotional discount strategies on retail beverage revenue using panel econometric methods.**

The dataset contains daily observations from 37 retail stores over approximately two years. A key challenge is cross-sectional dependence arising from common macroeconomic shocks that simultaneously affect all stores.

To address this issue, Common Correlated Effects Mean Group (CCEMG) estimators are applied to remove latent common factors before conducting causal analysis.

The study combines econometric theory, statistical inference, and machine learning to quantify both immediate and delayed revenue responses to promotional activity.

**Key Findings**

• Initial causality results were shown to be spurious due to cross-sectional dependence.

• CCEMG estimation successfully removed common-factor contamination.

• Discounts generated significant short-run revenue increases.

• Revenue gains were partially offset by negative effects in subsequent periods, indicating inter-temporal demand substitution.

• Hybrid LightGBM models further improved predictive performance by capturing nonlinear residual structure.
